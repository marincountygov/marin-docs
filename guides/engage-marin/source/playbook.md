# Part 1: Getting started

## About Engage Marin

Engage Marin is the County of Marin's online community engagement platform. It gives people a simple way to share ideas, give feedback, and help shape County decisions.

Engage Marin helps County agencies reach more people, hear different views, and learn what matters to the community.

You don't need to be an engagement expert to get started. The Engage Marin team can help you plan your approach, create content, and launch your conversation.

## How it works

Engage Marin helps you manage community engagement from start to finish. You can ask one question or bring several related questions together in a campaign.

1. **Plan.** Decide what you want to learn, who you want to hear from, and how you'll use their feedback.
2. **Create.** Start a question or campaign. Add an About page, welcome video, and kickoff update.
3. **Partner.** Work with trusted community groups to shape outreach and reach more people.
4. **Launch.** Publish your engagement and invite people through County and partner channels.
5. **Monitor.** Follow ideas, comments, votes, and participation.
6. **Organize.** Set themes to group related ideas and understand what people share.
7. **Update.** Post updates when appropriate to share progress and keep people informed.
8. **Close.** End participation, review results, and publish a recap with next steps.

A successful engagement doesn't end when people stop commenting. It ends when you show them what you learned and how their input will help.

## Your commitment

Engage Marin provides the tools and support. Your team brings the subject knowledge and stays involved throughout the conversation.

| Your team | Engage Marin team |
|---|---|
| Define the purpose | Help plan the engagement |
| Provide project details | Help create clear content |
| Identify community partners | Support partner setup |
| Promote participation | Provide tools and guidance |
| Follow the conversation | Support moderation |
| Review feedback | Provide themes and reports |
| Share results | Help close the engagement |

These are suggested roles. Confirm responsibilities with the Engage Marin team when planning your effort.

You don't need to do everything yourself. You do need someone on your team who can lead the effort, answer questions, and help turn feedback into next steps.

## Why partners matter

**Good engagement starts with trust.**

County announcements help spread the word, but trusted community partners can make the invitation more personal.

Partners may include nonprofits, neighborhood groups, service providers, schools, and other local organizations.

They help Engage Marin:

- **Reach more people.** Connect with communities that County channels may miss.
- **Build trust.** Invite people through groups and leaders they already know.
- **Encourage participation.** Explain why the topic matters to their communities.
- **Support inclusion.** Help people with different backgrounds and experiences take part.
- **Measure reach.** Use partner links to understand how people found the conversation.

### Partner early

Don't wait until your question is ready to publish.

Partners can help you understand what matters to their communities before you finalize the engagement.

Ask partners to help:

- Identify issues and concerns you may have missed.
- Review questions for clarity.
- Suggest ways to reach people.
- Share the invitation through trusted channels.
- Encourage people to take part.

Engage Marin gives partners their own links, logos, and welcome messages. This lets them invite people in their own voice while connecting everyone to the same conversation.

Partners do more than share a link. They help turn a County question into a community conversation.

## What to expect

Engage Marin makes community engagement easier, but a successful effort still takes planning, outreach, and follow-through.

Your team should plan to:

- Create a clear question and explain why it matters.
- Identify partners and plan outreach.
- Follow participation and share updates.
- Review feedback and identify common themes.
- Report back when the engagement closes.

The goal isn't just to collect ideas. It's to listen, learn, and show people how their input matters.

## Ready to start?

You don't need every detail worked out before reaching out.

Start with three questions:

1. **What do you want to learn?**
2. **Who do you want to hear from?**
3. **How will you use their feedback?**

The Engage Marin team can help you turn those answers into a community conversation.

# Part 2: Creating an engagement

## Campaigns

A campaign brings several related questions together under one larger effort.

Use a campaign when you want to explore different parts of the same issue.

For example:

**Campaign: Improving Marin transportation**

- How can we improve bus service?
- Where do we need safer crossings?
- How can we improve bike routes?

Each question collects its own ideas, comments, and votes. The campaign connects them under one effort.

**Use a question for one conversation. Use a campaign for several related conversations.**

Each campaign has its own landing page, title, description, and optional welcome video.

### Campaign title

Your title should tell people what the overall conversation is about.

Tips:

- **Keep it short.** Aim for 6 words or fewer.
- **Use plain language.** Avoid jargon and internal project names.
- **Cover the big picture.** Connect all questions in the campaign.
- **Make it inviting.** Encourage people to explore and participate.
- **Stay neutral.** Don't suggest a preferred outcome.

Examples:

- Envision Marin
- Reimagining the Civic Center
- Improving Transportation in Marin

### Campaign description

Give people enough context to understand why the questions belong together. Aim for **50–100 words**.

Include:

- **Why it matters.** Explain the purpose of the campaign.
- **What we're asking.** Describe the kinds of feedback you're seeking.
- **How it will help.** Explain how the County will use what it hears.

Tips:

- Start with the big picture.
- Don't repeat every question.
- Be clear about what is open for input.
- End with an invitation to explore the questions.

## Questions

A question is where people share ideas, comment, and vote.

You can create a standalone question or add it to a campaign. Each question has its own page, participation settings, updates, themes, and reports.

### Writing the question

Your question is the most important part of the conversation.

Tips:

- **Short.** Aim for 10 words or fewer.
- **Simple.** Use plain language.
- **Focused.** Ask one question.
- **Open.** Invite ideas, not yes/no answers.
- **Neutral.** Don't lead people toward a preferred answer.

Examples:

- What transportation improvements would make the biggest difference for you?
- How can we make County parks more welcoming for everyone?
- What should we prioritize in next year's budget?

### Question description

Use **2–4 short sentences** to give people enough context to respond.

Include:

- **Why it matters.** Explain why you're asking.
- **What you're asking.** Tell people what feedback you're looking for.
- **How it will help.** Explain how the County will use the feedback.

Tips:

- Use plain language.
- Avoid jargon and explain technical terms.
- End with an invitation to participate.

### Content builder

The Engage Marin content builder can help turn project notes into draft content.

It can help create:

- Campaign titles and descriptions.
- Questions and descriptions.
- About pages.
- First posts.
- Welcome video titles and descriptions.

Use it to get started, then review the content with your team before publishing.

## About page

Every question has an About page that gives people more background.

Aim for **150–300 words**.

Include:

- **What.** Explain the project, issue, or decision.
- **Why.** Explain why the County wants input.
- **Who.** Identify the team leading the effort.
- **When.** Share key dates and milestones.
- **Participation.** Explain what people can contribute.
- **Next steps.** Explain how the County will review and use feedback.

Tips:

- Start with the big picture.
- Use short paragraphs, headings, and bullets.
- Link to supporting documents.
- Be clear about what is and isn't open for input.
- Set realistic expectations about decisions and updates.

## Welcome video

A welcome video makes the conversation more personal and helps build trust. You can add videos to campaigns and questions.

Tips:

- **Keep it short.** Aim for 30–60 seconds.
- **Be yourself.** Speak naturally and look at the camera.
- **Explain why it matters.** Share the purpose and how feedback will help.
- **Invite everyone in.** Welcome different views and experiences.
- **End positively.** Thank people for sharing their ideas.

Avoid large blocks of text in videos. Use spoken words to explain the topic and invite participation.

### Video format

- Resolution: 1080p.
- Aspect ratio: 16:9 or 1:1.
- Preferred format: MP4.
- Captions: Required, using a reviewed WebVTT (.vtt) file.

Engage Marin videos are published through the County's YouTube channel.

### Video title

Keep the introduction title to **3–8 words**.

Start with an action when possible, clearly state the topic, and focus on the participant.

### Video description

Aim for **25–50 words** in 1–2 short sentences.

Include:

- **Who's speaking.** Name and title.
- **Why they're inviting participation.** Explain the purpose.
- **What people should do.** Invite viewers to contribute.

Keep it factual and conversational. Don't repeat everything said in the video.

## First post

Every question should begin with a welcome post. This starts the conversation and shows that someone is listening.

Aim for **75–150 words**.

Include:

- **Welcome.** Thank people for joining.
- **Why it matters.** Explain why the topic is important.
- **What we're asking.** Reinforce the question.
- **Participation.** Invite ideas, experiences, and questions.
- **Next steps.** Let people know what to expect.

Tips:

- Write like you're starting a conversation, not making an announcement.
- Be authentic and conversational.
- Focus on participants, not the County.
- Welcome different perspectives.
- End with an invitation to join the conversation.

## Community partners

Partners help bring more people into the conversation through trusted community connections.

Each partner can have a dedicated link, logo, and welcome message. People who use a partner link see that organization's connection to the engagement.

The platform can track ideas, comments, and votes that come through partner links. This helps show how partners contribute to participation.

### Working with partners

- Identify organizations that already reach your audience.
- Invite them early so they can help shape outreach.
- Let partners write their own welcome messages.
- Give them their dedicated links and outreach support.
- Share participation results and thank them for their support.

Partner links help measure participation, but they don't capture everyone who hears about the engagement through a partner.

## Outreach

Publishing your question is only the first step. Outreach helps people find it and understand why they should participate.

The County's Public Information Office can help:

- Identify communication channels.
- Develop or review promotional content.
- Prepare graphics and templates.
- Plan social media, newsletters, and in-person outreach.
- Consider translation and accessibility needs.

### Choosing channels

Use the channels that make sense for your audience.

Options include:

- County website and social media.
- County and district newsletters.
- Community organizations and trusted partners.
- Libraries, schools, and neighborhood groups.
- Community events, flyers, and direct outreach.
- Internal County communications, when appropriate.

## Before launch

Review the public experience before you publish.

### Readiness checklist

- [ ] We know what we want to learn.
- [ ] We know who we want to reach.
- [ ] We have a clear question or campaign.
- [ ] We have an About page and welcome post.
- [ ] Our welcome video works and has accurate captions, if used.
- [ ] We have identified community partners.
- [ ] We have an outreach plan.
- [ ] Participation dates and settings are correct.
- [ ] Required notices are in place.
- [ ] Partner links work.
- [ ] The page works on a phone.
- [ ] Someone will monitor activity and post updates.
- [ ] We have a plan to share results.

## During engagement

Once your question is live, people can share ideas, comment, and vote when those features are enabled.

Your team should stay involved.

### Monitor activity

Use Engage Marin's Overview and Reporting tools to follow participation.

Look at:

- Ideas submitted.
- Comments and votes.
- Participation over time.
- Topics drawing the most interest.
- Questions or concerns that may need a response.

### Set themes

Use Themes to group related ideas and understand common interests.

Themes can help you see what people are saying across many responses. Review them as participation grows and adjust them when needed.

### Post updates

Use Updates to keep people informed.

Share updates when:

- The engagement reaches an important milestone.
- You have new information to share.
- Several participants ask the same question.
- You want to encourage more participation.
- The engagement is nearing its close.

A question that receives regular, useful updates shows participants that the County is listening.

## Closing out

Closing a question ends new ideas, comments, and votes. People can still read the conversation.

Don't let the engagement end without explaining what happened.

### Closing the loop

A good closing recap answers three questions:

- **What did we hear?** Share the main ideas and different views.
- **What did we learn?** Explain what the feedback tells us.
- **What happens next?** Share next steps, decisions, or when people can expect another update.

### Review feedback

Look at the ideas, comments, votes, themes, and reports.

Identify common interests, different perspectives, and useful suggestions.

### Share results

Publish a recap that explains:

- **What we asked.** Remind people of the purpose.
- **Who participated.** Share available participation information.
- **What we heard.** Summarize common themes and different views.
- **What happens next.** Explain how the County will use the feedback.
- **When to expect updates.** Share the next milestone, if known.

If you created a campaign, close its questions and share an overall update.

### Thank participants

Thank residents and community partners for their time and contributions.

The most important part of engagement is closing the loop. People should know their feedback was heard, even when the County cannot act on every idea.

## Example engagement

This fictional example shows how the parts of an Engage Marin effort work together.

### Campaign

**Title:** Improving Marin parks

**Description:**

Marin's parks bring people together and offer places to play, relax, and enjoy nature. We want to learn how people use these spaces and what could make them better. Explore the questions below and share your ideas. Your feedback will help the County understand community priorities as it plans future park improvements.

### Question

**Question:** How can we make County parks more welcoming?

**Description:**

County parks serve people of all ages and backgrounds. We want to hear what works well and what could improve. Share your ideas to help us understand what matters most to park visitors.

### About page

**Overview**

County parks give people places to enjoy nature, spend time with family, and connect with their community.

We want to learn how people use these spaces and what could make them more welcoming.

**Why we're asking**

People visit parks for many reasons. Some come to walk or play. Others come to relax, meet friends, or enjoy nature.

We want to hear from people with different needs and experiences.

**How to participate**

Share what you enjoy about County parks and what you would change. You can also read other ideas, join discussions, and show support for suggestions.

**What happens next**

The County will review the feedback and look for common themes. What we learn will help inform future planning.

We'll share a summary of the results when the conversation closes.

### First post

**Welcome to the conversation!**

County parks are important places for our community. They give us room to play, relax, and enjoy the outdoors.

We want to hear your ideas for making these spaces more welcoming.

What do you enjoy most about County parks? What could make your visits better?

Share your ideas, read what others have posted, and join the discussion. We welcome different experiences and points of view.

We'll follow the conversation and share updates as we learn more.

Thank you for helping us make Marin's parks better for everyone.

### Partner invitation

**Example message for a community partner:**

We're working with Engage Marin to hear ideas about improving County parks. Your experiences and ideas matter. Join the conversation and help us understand what would make these spaces more welcoming for your community.

### Progress update

**Thank you for sharing!**

We've received ideas about park access, trails, play areas, and places to gather.

We're reviewing what people have shared and looking for common themes.

There's still time to join the conversation. We welcome more ideas and different perspectives.

### Closing recap

**What we heard**

Thank you to everyone who shared ideas about County parks.

Participants raised several common themes, including access, trails, play areas, and places to gather.

**What we learned**

People use parks in different ways. Their feedback helps us better understand what works and where improvements may help.

**What happens next**

The County will review these ideas as part of future park planning. We'll share more information as next steps become clear.

Thank you for helping shape the future of Marin's parks.

*These examples are for training only. They do not describe an active County project or actual engagement results.*

## Community guidelines

The Engage Marin Community Guidelines help create a respectful, welcoming, and productive space for everyone.

They explain how people can participate, what behavior is allowed, and how moderation works.

Our goal is to help people participate, not discourage them.

## Moderation

Engage Marin uses automated tools and human review to help keep conversations safe and respectful.

The platform checks submitted content for possible issues, such as harassment, threats, or harmful language.

A flag does not always mean someone broke the rules. For example, a person describing violence in their neighborhood may trigger a flag even when the comment is appropriate.

Depending on the content and context, a submission may:

- Be published.
- Need moderator review.
- Be removed if it violates the Community Guidelines.

Automated tools help identify possible problems. Human judgment remains important in deciding how to respond.

## Brand assets

Use approved Engage Marin logos, graphics, and templates when promoting your engagement.

The Engage Marin team and Public Information Office can help you find the right materials.
