Campaigns

A campaign brings several related questions together under one larger effort.

Updated October 29, 2026 at 1:47 PM PDT

Limits

ContentLimit
Campaign title6 words or fewer
Campaign description50–100 words

Use a campaign when you want to explore different parts of the same issue.

For example:

Campaign: Improving Marin transportation

  • How can we improve bus service?
  • Where do we need safer crossings?
  • How can we improve bike routes?

Each question collects its own ideas, comments, and votes. The campaign connects them under one effort.

Use a question for one conversation. Use a campaign for several related conversations.

Each campaign has its own landing page, title, description, and optional welcome video.

Campaign title

Your title should tell people what the overall conversation is about.

Tips:

  • Keep it short. Aim for 6 words or fewer.
  • Use plain language. Avoid jargon and internal project names.
  • Cover the big picture. Connect all questions in the campaign.
  • Make it inviting. Encourage people to explore and participate.
  • Stay neutral. Don't suggest a preferred outcome.

Examples:

  • Envision Marin
  • Reimagining the Civic Center
  • Improving Transportation in Marin

Campaign description

Give people enough context to understand why the questions belong together. Aim for 50–100 words.

Include:

  • Why it matters. Explain the purpose of the campaign.
  • What we're asking. Describe the kinds of feedback you're seeking.
  • How it will help. Explain how the County will use what it hears.

Tips:

  • Start with the big picture.
  • Don't repeat every question.
  • Be clear about what is open for input.
  • End with an invitation to explore the questions.
