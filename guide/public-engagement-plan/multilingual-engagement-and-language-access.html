Multilingual engagement and language access

Nearly one in five Marin residents is Hispanic, and a significant portion of the county's population speaks English less than very well.

Updated August 28, 2026 at 1:59 PM PDT

Download PDF

Two distinctions that matter

Translation is written. Documents, flyers, surveys, and materials converted from one language to another.

Interpretation is spoken. Live dialogue converted in real time. Different skills, different preparation, different logistics.

Interpretation lead times

Plan around these minimums. Rush requests are possible at additional cost. For the full request process, vendor contacts, and request forms, visit the Language Access Hub.

ServiceMinimum notice
Over the phone interpretationOn demand, 24/7
Video remote interpretationOn demand or scheduled
On-site consecutive interpretation72 hours
On-site simultaneous interpretation2 weeks; 3 weeks if equipment needed
Virtual simultaneous interpretation72 hours
ASL/CDI interpretationSee Language Access Hub
Document translationVaries by length; submit early
Required
  • AI translation does not meet the county's language access obligations. Qualified human interpreters are required at engagement events.
  • Language access costs and logistics must be built into the engagement plan before approval.
Best practice
  • Language varies within a language. Spanish in the Canal may differ from Spanish in West Marin's agricultural communities. Interpreters familiar with the specific community are almost always more effective.
  • Build a few minutes into the start of every multilingual meeting for a language access announcement. It signals that participants' language needs were anticipated, not addressed as an afterthought.
